# 人間中心設計に関連する規格

ここでは人間中心設計・ユーザビリティに関連するISO規格の番号と、その概要を示す。これらを理解して実践することが人間中心設計において重要である。

## ISO9241

### Overview:

The ISO 9241 series is a comprehensive set of international standards focusing on optimizing the ergonomics of human-system interaction. Originally titled "Ergonomic requirements for office work with visual display terminals (VDTs)," it has significantly evolved to encompass hardware, software, digital environments, accessibility, and design processes across modern digital products.

### Main Objective:

To improve usability, accessibility, and the overall user experience (UX) while reducing users' cognitive load, physical discomfort, and operational errors during interaction with technologies.

----

### ISO 9241-11：Usability: Definitions and concepts

Definition of Usability: "The extent to which a system, product or service can be used by specified users to achieve specified goals with effectiveness, efficiency and satisfaction in a specified context of use."

#### Three Key Usability Metrics:

- Effectiveness: The accuracy and completeness with which users achieve specified goals.
- Efficiency: The resources (e.g., time, effort, cost) expended in relation to the accuracy and completeness with which users achieve goals.
- Satisfaction: The extent to which user physical, cognitive, and emotional responses meet expectations.

Context of Use: Encompasses users, goals, tasks, resources, and physical/social environments.

### ISO 9241-110：Interaction principles

#### Seven Interaction Principles:

- 1.Suitability for the user's task: The system helps users complete tasks efficiently without unnecessary cognitive or operational friction.
- 2.Self-descriptiveness: The system's status and next possible steps are clear and require no external explanation.
- 3.Conformity with user expectations: The interaction is consistent and matches the user's mental models and common conventions.
- 4.Suitability for learning: The interface supports the user in discovering features and learning how to use the system.
- 5.Controllability: The user can start, pause, speed up, or alter the direction of the interaction.
- 6.Use-error robustness: The system prevents mistakes from occurring and minimizes the consequences of errors, allowing easy recovery.
- 7.Suitability for individualization: The system allows users to customize the interaction to match their individual needs or preferences.

### ISO 9241-112：Principles for the presentation of information

#### Six Principles for Presenting Information:

- 1.Detectability: Ensure that users can physically notice the presence of the presented information.
- 2.Freedom from distraction: Prevent users from being distracted from their focal task by irrelevant secondary information.
- 3.Discriminability: Enable users to easily distinguish one piece of information from another.
- 4.Unambiguous interpretability: Make sure the meaning of the presented information is clear and free from ambiguity.
- 5.Conciseness: Present only what is necessary, saving the user’s cognitive effort and reading time.
- 6.Consistency (Internal & External): Design information following consistent systems, layouts, and external standards.

### ISO 9241-210：Human-centred design for interactive systems

#### The 6 Principles of Human-Centred Design:

- 1.The design is based upon an explicit understanding of users, tasks and environments.

Designers must know exactly who will use the product, what they are trying to achieve, and where/how they will use it.

- 2.Users are involved throughout design and development.

Users shouldn't just be tested at the end of the project. They must be actively involved at every stage, from initial research to final testing.

- 3.The design is driven and refined by user-centred evaluation.

Design decisions are continuously validated and improved through user feedback and usability testing, not just internal opinions.

- 4.The process is iterative.

Design is not a straight line. Teams must build, test, learn, and repeat this cycle until the user's needs are fully met.

- 5.The design addresses the whole user experience.

A product must go beyond just usability (ease of use). It must consider the user's emotions, brand perception, and end-to-end journey.

- 6.The design team includes multidisciplinary skills and perspectives.

HCD requires a diverse team. Designers, engineers, researchers, and business stakeholders must collaborate to cover all perspectives.


#### The 4 Key Activities of the HCD Process:

- Activity 1.Understand and specify the context of use.

Identify the users, what they need to do, and the physical/social environment in which they operate.

- Activity 2.Specify the user and organizational requirements.

Determine exactly what the user needs the system to do to achieve their goals successfully.

- Activity 3.Produce design solutions (prototypes or wireframes).

Create concepts, wireframes, and prototypes that meet the specified requirements.

- Activity 4.Evaluating the design against requirements.

Test the prototypes with real users to see if the solutions meet their needs. If not, return to Activity 1, 2, or 3.
